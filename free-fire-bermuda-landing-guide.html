Bermuda Landing Guide: How to Pick a Drop for Your Playstyle

Learn how to evaluate landing locations, nearby routes, loot time and enemy traffic on Bermuda.

By Clutch Zone Editorial Team · Updated October 2026 · Practical player education

A good landing spot is not simply the place with the most loot. It is a place where your squad can collect enough equipment, understand nearby threats and leave with time to reach the next zone. The best choice depends on the flight path, your team’s comfort and whether you want a calm start or an early fight.

1. Evaluate the flight path and player traffic

Before jumping, look at the aircraft route and notice which locations are easy for many players to reach. A popular central area may attract several squads at once; a distant edge location may be quieter but can leave a longer rotation. Neither is automatically better. Make the choice intentionally and tell teammates the destination before anyone jumps.

2. Use a landing plan, not a vague marker

Choose a main landing point and a nearby fallback area. Squad members should land close enough to support one another without all entering the same small room. If enemies arrive first, regroup at the fallback instead of repeatedly re-entering the same fight one by one. If the area is clear, loot outward in a predictable pattern and keep track of where the rest of the team is.

3. Leave when the value of looting drops

Looting has diminishing returns. Once your team has workable weapons, ammunition and basic recovery supplies, extra searching may cost more than it gives. Check the zone and leave while there is still time to select a route. If you hear nearby combat, decide whether you have a real advantage before moving toward it; sound alone does not tell you enemy numbers or health.

4. Plan the first rotation while landing

Look for roads, open fields, ridgelines, buildings and other routes that could shape your movement. Mark a first regroup point so the team does not wander in different directions. When crossing exposed terrain, move from one cover option to the next rather than sprinting a long distance with no plan.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Jumping without confirming that teammates understood the destination.
  • Spreading too far apart to help one another during an early fight.
  • Staying to loot after the zone or enemy pressure makes the area unsafe.

A practical exercise

For several matches, use one planned landing area and record whether the squad had time to loot, regroup and rotate. Change one part of the plan at a time so you can learn which decision made the start more consistent.

Frequently asked questions

No. Match outcomes depend on opponents, game updates, device conditions and decisions. Use the guide as a practice framework and adapt it to the current game.

Should I copy another player’s exact setup?

Use other players’ approaches as ideas, not guarantees. Test changes safely on your own device and keep the settings and habits that improve your consistency.
